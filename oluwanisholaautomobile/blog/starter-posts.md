## 1. 5 Signs Your Transmission Is Failing

**Label:** `Blog`
**Meta description:** Slipping gears, delayed shifting or strange noises? Here are 5 warning signs of transmission trouble to watch for on Lagos roads, from Oluwanisholaautomobile, Mushin.

```html
<p>Your transmission works harder in Lagos traffic than almost anywhere else — constant stop-start driving, long idle periods, and heat all add up. Catching a transmission problem early can be the difference between a fluid service and a full rebuild. Here are five signs worth acting on.</p>

<h2>1. Slipping gears</h2>
<p>If your engine revs up but the car doesn't accelerate as it should, or you feel a delay before power kicks in, your transmission may be slipping. This is one of the earliest and most common warning signs, especially in heavy Mushin and Matori traffic where you're constantly shifting between low gears.</p>

<h2>2. Delayed or rough gear changes</h2>
<p>A healthy automatic transmission shifts smoothly and without you noticing. A jerk, clunk, or noticeable pause when changing gear — particularly from park into drive — usually points to worn components or degraded transmission fluid.</p>

<h2>3. Unusual noises</h2>
<p>Whining, humming, or clunking sounds that change with engine speed or gear position are a sign something inside the transmission needs attention. Manual transmissions that grind when shifting are telling you the same thing in a different way.</p>

<h2>4. Burning smell or leaking fluid</h2>
<p>Transmission fluid should be reddish and clean-smelling. A burnt smell, or fluid spots under your car, usually means the fluid is old, low, or contaminated — all of which accelerate wear on internal components.</p>

<h2>5. Warning light or won't go into gear</h2>
<p>Modern cars often flag transmission issues through the dashboard before you feel them. If a warning light comes on, or your car refuses to engage a gear at all, don't keep driving — get it scanned.</p>
```

## 2. How Lagos Heat Affects Your Car's Air Conditioning System

**Label:** `Blog`
**Meta description:** Lagos heat and traffic put extra strain on your car's A/C system. Here's why it fails faster here, and how to keep it running cold — from Oluwanisholaautomobile.

```html
<p>If you drive in Lagos, your air conditioning isn't optional — it's the difference between a bearable commute and a miserable one, especially crawling through Mushin traffic in the afternoon sun. But Lagos conditions also wear A/C systems out faster than milder climates. Here's why, and what to watch for.</p>

<h2>Why heat is hard on your A/C</h2>
<p>Constant high ambient temperatures mean your compressor runs longer and harder to keep the cabin cool, especially in slow traffic where there's less airflow over the condenser. Over time, this extra load accelerates wear on seals, hoses, and the compressor itself, making refrigerant leaks and component failures more common than in cooler climates.</p>

<h2>Common signs your A/C needs attention</h2>
<ul>
<li>Airflow that's weak even at the highest fan setting</li>
<li>Air that's cool but not cold, especially at idle or in traffic</li>
<li>A hissing or clicking noise when the A/C is switched on</li>
<li>A musty smell from the vents (often a sign of a dirty cabin filter or evaporator)</li>
</ul>

<h2>What we check</h2>
<p>When you bring your car in for an A/C issue, we check refrigerant levels, look for leaks around hoses and seals, test the compressor and blower motor, and inspect the cabin filter. Many weak-cooling complaints are a simple regas or filter change; others point to a leak or failing compressor that needs proper repair.</p>

<h2>A quick tip between services</h2>
<p>Running your A/C for a few minutes even on cooler days helps keep seals lubricated and can prevent the system drying out and leaking — a common cause of "my A/C stopped working after the rainy season" complaints we hear.</p>
```

```html
<h2>What a diagnostic scan actually does</h2>
<p>Using a computerized scan tool, we connect to your car's onboard computer and read live and stored fault codes across all major systems — engine, transmission, ABS, airbags, and more on most modern vehicles. <p>Sensors can drift out of range, catalytic converters can start losing efficiency, and minor electrical faults can develop gradually — all without an obvious symptom at first. <h2>What we check</h2>
```

```html
<p>Engine performance and noise, transmission behavior, brakes, suspension and steering — checked both at rest and on a short test drive where possible.</p>
```
